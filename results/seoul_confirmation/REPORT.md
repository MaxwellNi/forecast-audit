# Seoul hourly forecasting study

This locked historical reanalysis provides a raw-data-to-forecast-to-heldout-risk example. On the Ridge baseline, the selected augmentation improves confirmation MSE from 53,554 to 31,123, but gating selects exactly the same forecast as ungated augmentation. On the seasonal baseline, the gate rejects every candidate and incurs MSE 333,914, while ordinary blending reaches 40,231. Both branches and all adverse results remain in the record.

The source is the [UCI Seoul Bike Sharing Demand dataset](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/560/seoul+bike+sharing+demand), DOI [10.24432/C5F62R](https://doi.org/10.24432/C5F62R), with 8,760 hourly rental-count records. The unchanged provider download is included; [DATA_LICENSE.md](DATA_LICENSE.md) gives CC BY 4.0 attribution and transformation details. This is a new source within the study inventory, rather than a third-party replication, an externally registered protocol, a future-live deployment, or an independent sample of hours.

Release preparation normalized file-location descriptions in the code and protocol and updated their receipt links. [ORIGINAL_RUN_HASHES.json](ORIGINAL_RUN_HASHES.json) retains the original identities. Recorded timestamps below describe that original run; the revised public script and receipt bytes were not executed at those timestamps. Source data, fitted models, candidate CSV and every confirmation result retain their original bytes. No numerical or statistical procedure was changed by packaging.

The forecast is one hour ahead under the explicit operational assumption that the preceding hourly count is available. Inputs comprise lags 1, 2, 3, 24, 25, 48 and 168, past-only 24-hour and 168-hour means, and target-calendar harmonics/weekend. Contemporary weather, holiday indicators, functioning-day status and current/future rental counts are excluded. No predictor is generated by injecting an outcome innovation. Historical data publication vintages and actual reporting latency are not reconstructed. Prior outcomes from the confirmation window can enter subsequent lag features, exactly as in sequential one-step forecasting; they never update model weights or a choice.

All source rows, including operational-closure zeros, are retained. This matters because a lag-based method cannot automatically anticipate a newly started closure. Fixed calendar windows also expose seasonal change rather than assuming stationarity. A timing check modified every current/future count at three cutoffs by one million and confirmed that all forecasts and residual predictors at or before each cutoff were unchanged; [forecast_timing_check.json](forecast_timing_check.json) was written before confirmation.

## Estimand, baselines and fair comparators

The scale is **raw rental counts**; MSE and gains have units of rental-count squared, and MAE has units of rental counts. There is no rank-to-raw-risk conversion.

The two prespecified baseline controls are the previous-day forecast and a Ridge model using the lag/calendar features. Forecast models train only in the first window. For each baseline `B`, the second window fits `mY(B)` and each `mX(B)` on an intercept, linear baseline term and cubic B-splines with five quantile knots. The nuisance functions are frozen estimated projections, **not known population conditional means**. The reported baseline is `mY(B)`; direct unadjusted baseline risks are also recorded.

Each baseline has the same eight declared candidates: previous hour, previous day, previous week, Ridge, histogram gradient boosting, a reflected-Ridge control, an exact baseline copy, and an independent-noise perturbation of the baseline. Duplicate/degenerate candidates stay in the complete family. The reflected and noise controls are diagnostic models, not asserted zero-target truths on unknown real data.

For residual `rX=X-mX(B)`, the selection window fixes `t=max(mean(rX*(Y-mY)),0)/mean(rX^2)`, with `t=0` for exact copies or degenerate residual variance. Three strategies then independently minimize selection MSE, with the baseline available to each:

1. **Conventional convex blend:** `mY+a*(X-mY)`, fitting `a` in `[0,1]`.
2. **Ungated residual augmentation:** `mY+t*rX`.
3. **Audit-gated residual augmentation:** the same augmentation, restricted to candidates passing the fixed gate; otherwise use `mY`.

The gate applies a nominal one-sided Student reference to eight weekly residual-product means, using lag-one Bartlett HAC and an 8/7 correction, then full-family BY at 0.05 across all **16** baseline/candidate pairs. This is a temporal diagnostic, **not a claim of calibrated real-data FDR**. One candidate passes: HGB relative to Ridge, with BY value 0.001866. HGB relative to the seasonal baseline has nominal p=0.006677 but BY value 0.109404 and is not retained. The short selection window and family penalty have material consequences.

The direct models prevent attributing a weak-baseline improvement entirely to the audit. Direct HGB achieves MSE **40,231.11**, Ridge **59,688.91**, previous hour **98,309.00**, previous day **423,441.35**, and previous week **458,293.76**. This study shows useful learned augmentation; it does **not** show that the gate causes its improvement.

## Learned coefficient and the gain identity

For fixed learned `mY,mX,t`, the empirical algebra is exactly

`gain = 2*t*theta_confirm - t^2*v_confirm`.

This applies to the frozen projected residuals. The data do not reveal `theta_infinity`, true conditional means, or the population-optimal coefficient. The output field containing `confirm_oracle_gain_diagnostic_only` is the **ex-post empirical optimum on the confirmation residuals**, reported for diagnosis only, not a population oracle and never used for selection or deployment.

| HGB relative to | Frozen `t` | Selection plug-in gain | Realized augmentation gain | Ex-post empirical confirmation optimum |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Previous day | 1.050546 | 131,425.80 | 299,789.52 | 300,149.53 |
| Ridge | 0.786989 | 13,808.81 | 22,431.43 | 23,058.38 |

For Ridge, the ex-post empirical coefficient is 0.942380; the squared coefficient mismatch accounts for 626.95 count-squared of gain relative to that empirical optimum. The plug-in gain is not a calibrated forecast of realized gain. For the seasonal branch, the large potential augmentation gain is rejected by the gate, so **the actual gated-choice gain remains zero**. Predictions are intentionally unclipped to preserve the quadratic identity; negative predictions occur for 3.57% of the selected Ridge augmentation. Their rate is reported for every forecast. No favorable clipping variant was selected after confirmation.

## Verification and interpretation

Run `python verify.py` to check all public receipt hash links, original source/model/result identities, chronological ordering of the recorded timestamps, complete-family BY, frozen selection choices, 16 empirical gain identities, 74 risks from 888 weekly records, and 82 paired gains and weekly HAC standard errors.

Run `python verify_predictions.py` to additionally verify every current manifest entry, reconstruct all 74 forecasts from the unchanged models, check all 82 weekly/fortnight/bootstrap comparisons, and perturb current/future outcomes at three confirmation cutoffs. It verifies the released feature path on already exposed data; it does not retroactively establish the historical timing check or external preregistration. Default verification is read-only. The recorded results are [verification.json](verification.json) and [prediction_verification.json](prediction_verification.json).

The field `confirm_oracle_gain_diagnostic_only`, and its coefficient counterpart `oracle_coefficient_diagnostic_only`, contain an ex-post empirical optimum on the fixed confirmation residuals. They do not contain a population oracle and were never deployed or used for selection. Forecast reconstruction checks empirical arithmetic, rather than population conditional means or the calibration of temporal intervals.

This study supports useful fitted residual augmentation on this support and records a failure of gate-based choice. It does not establish gating superiority, calibrated FDR on dependent hours, joint interval coverage, or broad cross-domain utility.
